Why a sphere?

The momentum transfer is q = kout − kin. A periodic crystal reinforces scattering when q equals a reciprocal-lattice vector G. Elastic scattering also requires |kout| = |kin|. The Ewald sphere collects the outgoing wavevectors allowed by that last condition.

q = G   and   |kin + G| = |kin|

The diagram is scaled by 2π/d. The reciprocal vectors have integer coordinates, and the incident wavevector length is d/λ = 1/(λ/d). Moving the wavelength slider therefore changes the sphere's radius.

This is an ideal geometric construction for a simple-cubic lattice and monochromatic elastic scattering. Points are exact reciprocal-lattice points; the visual proximity band is not a physical peak width. We do not calculate structure factors, finite-crystal peak shapes, dynamical scattering, measured intensities or detector resolution.
